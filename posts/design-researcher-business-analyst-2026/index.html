Who we are

About OpenCRVS

Since 2018, we have been building OpenCRVS: free, open-source software for civil registration and vital statistics (CRVS) that helps governments register births, deaths and other life events. OpenCRVS is a not-for-profit initiative. It is a digital public good, recognised by the Digital Public Goods Alliance.

Without a birth certificate, a child does not exist in the eyes of the law. Around 1 in 4 children under five worldwide are still unregistered, and over 100 countries lack a fully functioning CRVS system. OpenCRVS is built to change that. It works offline and on modest hardware, and it is designed around the people who use it every day.

Sources: UNICEF, Birth Registration for Every Child by 2030: Are We on Track?, 2019 (1 in 4 children under five) · World Bank and WHO, Global Civil Registration and Vital Statistics Scaling Up Investment Plan 2015–2024, 2014 (over 100 countries) · DPG Registry, digitalpublicgoods.net/r/opencrvs

We currently support projects in the Philippines, Burkina Faso, Uganda, Somalia and the Pacific Islands. Learn more about our UN Development Partnership, our technical documentation and our code on GitHub.

Why it matters

The role

Each country's civil registration system is shaped by its own laws, institutions and practice. Every OpenCRVS implementation goes through three stages: discovery, a country requirements specification, and configuration. We learn how vital events are declared, validated, registered and certified in that country, document the requirements against our library of civil registration business rules, then configure OpenCRVS to meet them. What we learn in each country also shapes the core product.

As our Design Researcher / Business Analyst, you will lead discovery and requirements gathering for country implementations, and turn what you find into a specification our configuration team can build from.

You will work with country Project Managers, our Product Owner, Design Lead, product designers, developers and QA testers, and directly with ministry officials, registrars and registration staff in partner countries. You will be part of a 30-strong team based across Europe (Paris, London, Helsinki and Lisbon).

Responsibilities

What you will do

Plan and run discovery

  • Plan discovery for each country: research questions, stakeholder map, sampling, screeners, interview and field guides, and a schedule agreed with the country team.
  • Do field research with the people who declare and register vital events, including in remote and low-connectivity areas.
  • Plan, facilitate and document requirements workshops and interviews with senior officials, registrars and partner organisations.

Understand the law and the process

  • Review civil registration legislation, regulations and policy, plus existing forms, registers and certificates.
  • Map the current process for each vital event in scope: actors, offices, steps, documents, data flows, fees and time limits.
  • Agree the target process in OpenCRVS with the country.

Specify what we build

  • Write the country requirements specification against our business rule library: forms, workflows, user roles and permissions, certificates, notifications and reporting.
  • Find gaps against OpenCRVS core and work with the Product Owner to turn them into backlog tickets with acceptance criteria.
  • Use and build on our internal AI tooling to gather requirements and write specifications.

Test, share and improve

  • Run usability testing with end users, and support user acceptance testing with QA testers and country stakeholders.
  • Turn research into insights, personas and journey maps, and present them in writing, visuals and workshops.
  • Improve the templates, questions and methods we use for discovery and requirements across countries.
